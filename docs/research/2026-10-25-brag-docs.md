# Brag docs (accomplishment logs for performance reviews)

A **brag document** (also called a hype document or “list of stuff I did”) is a maintained, fact-based record of your work contributions, impact, collaboration, and learning—used both to help managers and peers advocate for you in performance reviews/promotions and to reflect on themes and goals. The practice was popularized in engineering circles by Julia Evans’ 2019 first-party guide on jvns.ca; earlier she described a related career-narrative write-up (2017). Closely related first-party artifacts include Will Larson’s Staff **promotion packet** (started early “much the way they might use a brag document”) and Aashni Shah’s **Hype Doc** / personal-brand tooling. Primary sources emphasize outcomes over activity dumps, sharing the document with advocates, and including “fuzzy” or invisible work that otherwise falls under the radar.

---

## Key practices

- **Write it down so memory isn’t the system of record.** Performance-review time often feels like “wait, what did I do in the last 6 months?” even when substantial work happened; people forget mentoring, small security projects, migration help, design-doc contributions, etc. ([jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/))
- **Assume your manager also forgets.** Managers must explain promotions or ratings to others; “X’s work is so awesome!!!!” is not enough—they need concrete material. ([jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/))
- **Share with your manager** so they can advocate prepared: Evans reports managers are thankful because the doc makes writing the review easier than reconstructing from memory. Especially valuable on **manager transitions** before a review. ([jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/))
- **Share with peer reviewers** when peer feedback is part of perf/promo; goals in the doc also cue where feedback is most useful. Some teams make sharing a team norm. ([jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/))
- **Don’t just list tasks—attach impact.** Pair reviews catch under-bragging: people write “I shipped $feature” and miss “… which caused $thing to happen.” Prefer facts that sound “exactly as good as [the work] is,” e.g. primary contributor to a feature “now used by 60% of our customers.” ([jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/))
- **Explain the big picture / themes.** Group work under focus areas (security, product skills, code-review culture) so fuzzy, multi-small-action efforts read as a coherent narrative. ([jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/))
- **Include fuzzy / invisible work deliberately**—code quality, on-call ease, fairer interview/perf process, tech debt—because it is “the most likely to fall under the radar.” For each goal: (1) why it matters, (2) actions toward it, (3) effects seen, even if indirect. ([jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/))
- **Use the doc for reflection, not only promotion.** Evans says the ideas have been “more useful… to help me reflect on themes… what’s important to me, what I’m learning, and what I’d like to be doing differently” than for raises alone. Prompt examples: pride, themes, more/less of, intended vs actual effect, what to do differently. ([jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/))
- **Prefer a fact-based register over self-grading.** In the 2017 precursor write-up: “we did X! here is the reason we did it! here were the results!” instead of “I did a good job.” ([jvns.ca/blog/2017/03/17/career-narrative/](https://jvns.ca/blog/2017/03/17/career-narrative/))
- **Go back later and fill in post-launch results.** “It’s often important to go back a few months later and fill in what actually happened after you launched the project.” ([jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/))
- **Treat peer praise to managers as complementary evidence.** “You can work on this for yourself by writing a brag document, but having statements from coworkers… really helps build credibility.” Ask before praising someone to their manager. ([jvns.ca/blog/2020/07/14/when-your-coworker-does-great-work-tell-their-manager/](https://jvns.ca/blog/2020/07/14/when-your-coworker-does-great-work-tell-their-manager/))
- **Record tiny learning milestones** in the brag doc if you don’t blog—skills progress is easy to lose track of. ([jvns.ca/blog/2022/03/13/celebrate-tiny-learning-milestones/](https://jvns.ca/blog/2022/03/13/celebrate-tiny-learning-milestones/))
- **For Staff-plus paths, start a promotion packet early** “much the way they might use a brag document,” so the packet becomes “the map to accomplishing your goal,” then later edit down into the company’s formal template. ([staffeng.com/guides/promo-packets/](https://staffeng.com/guides/promo-packets/); [lethain.com/promo-packets/](https://lethain.com/promo-packets/))
- **Candidate-led promo writing (where packets exist):** ~80% authored by the candidate, manager as editor/coach/advocate—because “no one knows the work you’ve done or the impact you’ve had better than you do.” ([philosopherdeveloper.com](https://philosopherdeveloper.com/posts/the-case-for-writing-your-own-promo))
- **Personal brand / Hype Doc adjacent practice (Shah):** experience maps and hype tooling are for communicating unique value; an experience map “shouldn’t just list your education and professional milestones—that’s what your resume is for.” ([Medium: Aashni Shah](https://medium.com/@aashnisshah/hype-yourself-youre-worth-it-efb108def0d); recommended by Evans)

## Recommended structure / sections

### Julia Evans’ brag document template (one doc per year; “5-10 pages or more” OK)

Cited from [jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/):

| Section | What to capture |
| --- | --- |
| **Goals for this year** | Major goals; sharing helps others support you |
| **Goals for next year** | Near year-end, draft next year’s goals |
| **Projects** | Per project: your contributions (design? components? key insights like cutting scope); impact (who for; dollars saved; % improvement; users; audit/retention non-numeric goals); update results after launch |
| **Collaboration & mentorship** | Expert help, mentoring, clear emails/notes, foundational code others built on, monitoring/on-call, notable code review, cross-team answers, project advice, internal talks |
| **Design & documentation** | Design docs written/reviewed; docs with goal (e.g. reduce repeated questions) |
| **Company building** | Interviewing/recruiting, process improvements, onboarding materials—company-wide, not just team delivery |
| **What you learned** | Skills acquired (perf analysis, internals, libraries, tools, domains, PM/UX, design writing, languages) |
| **Outside of work** | Blog posts, talks, OSS, industry recognition; optional personal wins |

**Stuck prompts:** “If you were trying to convince a friend to come join your company/team, what would you tell them about your work?” / “Did anybody tell you that you did something well recently?”

### 2017 career-narrative format (precursor)

Sections used with manager help: Projects; Design work & documentation; Collaboration & mentorship; “Stuff that isn’t my job.” Split by year; honest about whether projects worked as hoped. ([jvns.ca/blog/2017/03/17/career-narrative/](https://jvns.ca/blog/2017/03/17/career-narrative/))

### Will Larson Staff promotion packet (related, promo-optimized)

Keep project blurbs “very short” and link design docs; cover org-level leverage, quantifiable impact, mentoring outcomes, **glue work** + impact, named advocates, and **real or perceived gaps** with one-sentence mitigations. ([staffeng.com/guides/promo-packets/](https://staffeng.com/guides/promo-packets/))

### Lara Hogan leadership-impact prompts (for fuzzy leadership work)

Useful fill-ins when IC-style “shipped X” metrics don’t fit: clarity/predictability created; teammates’ skill/career help; decisions facilitated and blockers/missteps avoided; “What will the future look like, thanks to the work you’ve done?” ([larahogan.me/blog/help-your-teammates-navigate-self-doubt/](https://larahogan.me/blog/help-your-teammates-navigate-self-doubt/))

## Cadence and process

| Practice | Source |
| --- | --- |
| **Update every ~2 weeks** (many people) *or* a **marathon every 6–12 months** looking through PRs, tickets, launch emails, design docs (Evans’ preferred personal cadence). “Try out different approaches.” | [jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/) |
| **Usually one brag document per calendar year** (e.g. “Julia’s 2017 brag document”). | same |
| **Brag workshop** before review season: (1) 1–2h solo draft from artifacts; (2) 1h paired review to strengthen impact statements. Originated to help people who undersell their work. | same |
| **Biweekly group writing sessions** (can be multi-company, remote) to celebrate and update together. | same |
| **Colleague nudges:** “hey, you should put that in your brag doc”—social reinforcement as team norm. | same |
| **Promo packet loop:** empty packet → manager alignment → draft → wait two days → peer edit → manager gap review → periodic 1:1 steering (especially across manager changes). Formal company format comes late via edit-down. | [staffeng.com/guides/promo-packets/](https://staffeng.com/guides/promo-packets/) |
| **~20 minutes of focused reflection** with impact questions when processing privately. | [larahogan.me](https://larahogan.me/blog/help-your-teammates-navigate-self-doubt/) |

## Common failure modes (from primary sources)

- **Relying on memory at review time** → forgotten important work; demoralizing false sense of emptiness. ([jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/))
- **Listing ships without outcomes** (“I shipped $feature” without the follow-up impact). Caught in paired brag workshops. (same)
- **Omitting fuzzy / glue / invisible work** because it’s hard to quantify—exactly the work most likely to be missed. (same; glue also called out in Larson)
- **Underselling** relative to actual importance of the work (workshop motivation: encourage people to “brag” a little). (same)
- **Over-polishing beyond the facts**—Evans: you don’t need to make work sound better than it is; make it sound as good as it is. (same)
- **Self-evaluation as vague judgment** instead of facts and results. ([career narrative](https://jvns.ca/blog/2017/03/17/career-narrative/))
- **Waiting until Staff nomination to start a packet**—then archival scramble instead of continuous steering. ([promo packets](https://staffeng.com/guides/promo-packets/))
- **Manager-written-only promo packets** that leave the candidate passive and underinformed about level criteria. ([philosopherdeveloper](https://philosopherdeveloper.com/posts/the-case-for-writing-your-own-promo))
- **Unsolicited praise to someone’s manager** that misaligns with their goals/level or exposes “wrong” work—ask first. ([tell their manager](https://jvns.ca/blog/2020/07/14/when-your-coworker-does-great-work-tell-their-manager/))
- **Goal framing that ignores actual learning path**—celebrate milestones in the brag doc rather than only failed original goals. ([tiny learning milestones](https://jvns.ca/blog/2022/03/13/celebrate-tiny-learning-milestones/))

## What is *not* a brag doc (and adjacent distinctions)

Primary sources rarely use a crisp “vs resume / vs status report” checklist; distinctions below are grounded where authors draw lines, then flagged when only secondary popularizations emphasize them.

| Artifact | How primary sources relate it | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Resume** | Shah: experience maps shouldn’t be “just… education and professional milestones—that’s what your resume is for.” | Brag docs are longer, internal, period-scoped, evidence-rich. |
| **Promotion / self-review packet** | Larson: company formal packet is a *later* edit of material collected continuously; Evans’ brag doc feeds manager/peer advocacy for perf. Candidate should often author most of the promo case. | Related but not identical: packets map to level rubrics and may include **gaps**. |
| **Judgmental self-grade** | Evans 2017 prefers facts/results over “I did a good job.” | Brag = evidence register + narrative, not a score. |
| **Activity / journal dump** | Implied by impact requirements and fuzzy-work structure (goal → actions → effects), not “everything I did today.” | Explicit “not a daily journal” language is common in **secondary** guides (e.g. productized “brag doc” blogs); treat as consistent with Evans’ impact emphasis, not as her wording. |
| **Status report** | Not named by Evans. Status reports are audience-facing progress for stakeholders; brag docs optimize for career evidence and reflection. | Distinction mostly in secondary sources. |

**Nuance:** Evans’ brag document is **meant to be shared** with managers and peers—not framed as forever-private scratch notes. Secondary blogs that insist “private only / never for anyone else” diverge from the original practice (though a private capture layer that you later curate for sharing is compatible).

## Disagreements / tensions between sources

1. **Cadence:** Evans endorses both biweekly updates *and* rare marathon sessions; later practitioner essays often prescribe daily notebook → quarterly rollup. No primary “must” cadence.
2. **Audience:** Evans centers sharing for advocacy; many secondary “brag doc” explainers center privacy until review season.
3. **Length & compression:** Evans accepts long yearly docs (5–10+ pages); Larson wants Staff project writeups “very short” with links—promo packets are curated maps, not exhaustive logs.
4. **Gaps & level mapping:** Larson explicitly inventories skill/behavior gaps and level criteria; Evans’ template centers goals, projects, collaboration, learning—not gap analysis.
5. **Primary purpose:** Evans elevates reflection/themes as often *more* useful than promotion; Larson and Atlassian-style promo writing optimize for committee evidence and trajectory.
6. **Naming:** “brag document,” “hype document,” “list of stuff I did,” promo packet, career narrative—overlapping practices with different formality.
